\ABSTRACT{%
Modern compute clusters serve jobs whose rates depend on co-location, host
load, accelerator memory, and placement.  This makes the natural scheduling
action a full configuration rather than a scalar resource allocation.  We model
the problem as a configuration-action stochastic processing network and prove a
robust candidate MaxWeight theorem.  The theorem keeps the full action space in
the capacity statement and charges four implementable losses against explicit
slack: candidate approximation, conservative service estimation, bounded
penalties, and approximate optimization.  A positive residual margin gives a
finite-set Foster drift certificate under bounded conditional second moments.
We construct candidate families from finite feature cells, obtaining an
explicit cardinality bound and an \(L\rho\) support-loss certificate, and derive
finite-horizon expected-backlog bounds in addition to recurrence.  We provide a
Lean formalization of the fixed-family, queue-indexed statewise, candidate-cover,
diagonal-scaling, and variable-duration frame statements.
We instantiate the model in Scheduleurm, an operational research-cluster scheduler.  The
implementation records measured lower-service rows, admissible candidate
configurations, penalty terms, oracle gaps, and production-admission status.
Across declared finite service slices and 192 Poisson and bursty replay
scenarios, the robust candidate improves geometric-mean makespan by
\(1.61\times\) and mean flow by \(4.02\times\) over legacy Scheduleurm caps.
On the same measured service cache, it is not Pareto-dominated by the registered
policy-semantics baselines used to represent throughput, delay, and
interference-control schedulers.  The evidence supports a measured finite-slice
stability and performance claim, with external full-stack and future-workload
generalization left outside the main claim.
}%

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering

Schedulers for modern research clusters operate in a regime that is poorly
described by a fixed resource vector.  A reinforcement learning (RL) job can
retain near-solo progress when several copies share a graphics processing unit
(GPU), whereas a dense GPU kernel can lose finite-batch delay performance when
the same GPU is aggressively multiplexed.  Central processing unit (CPU)-heavy
evaluation jobs stress host cores and memory rather than accelerator compute,
and short control-plane jobs expose queueing overhead and fragmentation.  These
cases are not exceptions to a simple model; they are the model.  The scheduler
chooses global configurations whose service rates depend on the set of jobs,
their placement, co-location profile, and local hardware state.

The operations-research difficulty is that the natural action is a global
configuration.  Enumerating all placements and co-location patterns is
combinatorial, but reducing the problem to a small list of hand-picked
``sweet spots'' can invalidate stability claims.  A policy may look efficient on
a finite batch while silently using an infeasible profile, or it may be
throughput-optimal in a model that is disconnected from the measured service
map.  Our starting point is therefore to keep the full action family in the
mathematical statement and to treat the implemented candidate family as an
approximation whose loss must be paid from explicit slack.
Figure~\ref{fig:core-certificate} gives the one-page certificate structure:
candidate construction, lower-service scoring, slack accounting, and bounded
claim boundaries are checked as separate objects.

The main theoretical contribution is a robust candidate MaxWeight theorem with
complete slack accounting.  The theorem states exactly how candidate
approximation, conservative service estimation, scheduling penalties, and
approximate optimization consume capacity slack.  When the remaining margin is
positive and the stochastic primitives have bounded conditional second moments,
the resulting policy satisfies finite-horizon expected-backlog bounds and a
finite-set Foster recurrence certificate.  A finite feature partition provides
a constructive candidate family whose size is bounded by the number of active
cells and whose support loss is at most \(L\rho\).  The stability result is
stated for both fixed feasible families and state-dependent feasible families,
the latter being the case relevant to live schedulers whose feasible actions
depend on available jobs, alive nodes, and measured capacity boundaries.
For migration and other event-driven actions, a separate variable-duration
frame theorem preserves the same per-time slack accounting in cumulative
service units and gives an embedded-chain recurrence certificate.

The empirical contribution is a theorem-condition evaluation, not a claim that a
single implementation has solved cluster scheduling in full generality.  We
instantiate the theorem in Scheduleurm, an existing scheduler used for local
research workloads, and report bounded pieces of evidence.  First, measured
service curves close declared finite slices for four workload buckets: local
light-control tasks, GPU-heavy JAX numerical-computing matrix-multiplication
tasks, local CPU-heavy tasks, and Robust-Ensemble Soft Actor-Critic (RE-SAC) /
Bayesian Amnesic Piecewise-Robust (BAPR)-like hybrid RL tasks.  Second,
explicit replay compares robust candidate actions with legacy rules and
diagnostic policy families on a common measured service cache, so all policies
are evaluated against the same lower-service object.  Third, holdout
diagnostics, finite lower-service capacity certificates, ablation, production
coverage, and live-state dry-run trace checks identify which assumptions are
certified and which remain finite-slice claims.  These results connect the
proof obligations to scheduler artifacts; they do not replace a universal
fabric-cover theorem or an exhaustive full-stack scheduler competition.
Frozen FJSP, MMRCPSP, and port holdouts additionally test whether the finite
trajectory-action interface survives domain-specific feasibility and
reconfiguration constraints; they are portability evidence, not automatic
transfer of the server stochastic model.

The paper is organized as follows.  We first state the claim scope and evidence
contract, then position the work relative to scheduler and queueing literatures.
We then introduce the configuration-action processing network, give the
candidate approximation and robust MaxWeight stability theorem, describe the
Scheduleurm instantiation, report the computational evidence, and close with
the remaining calibration limits.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Deep-learning and heterogeneous cluster schedulers}
\label{subsec:related-systems}

Goodput-oriented deep-learning schedulers such as Gavel, Pollux, and Sia use
measured or modeled throughput tables to allocate heterogeneous accelerators
\citep{narayanan2020gavel,qiao2021pollux,sia2023}.  Fairness-oriented systems
such as Themis study efficient sharing under fairness constraints
\citep{mahajan2020themis}.  GPU sharing systems such as Gandiva, Salus, and
IADeep expose or exploit fine-grained co-location and interference behavior
\citep{xiao2018gandiva,yu2020salus,iadeep2023}.  Learning-based schedulers such
as Decima and DL2 show another way to adapt scheduling decisions from traces or
reinforcement learning \citep{mao2019decima,peng2019dl2}.  These systems
motivate our measured-service replay baselines, but our theoretical question is
different: we ask how a scheduler can restrict a combinatorial configuration
action space to a candidate family while preserving a queueing-stability
certificate with explicit loss terms.

Hybrid CPU/GPU placement and cluster allocation systems such as AlloX and
related heterogeneous placement work also emphasize that accelerator scheduling
cannot be reduced to GPU count alone \citep{le2020allox,carvalho2024workload}.
Scheduleurm follows the same empirical lesson.  Its q00, q10, and q11 buckets
are declared local or node-bucket service certificates, not claims that all
CPU-heavy or RL workloads have the same service curve.

\subsection{Queueing-network control and learning}
\label{subsec:related-queueing}

MaxWeight and backpressure policies are classical tools for stabilizing
constrained queueing systems under capacity-region slack
\citep{tassiulas1992stability,stolyar2004maxweight,neely2010stochastic}.
Foster-Lyapunov and fluid-limit arguments also underlie positive recurrence and
moment control in multiclass queueing networks
\citep{dai1995stability,meyn2009markov}.  Our proof follows this lineage but
adds three implementation-facing terms: candidate-set approximation,
lower-service estimation, and approximate-oracle loss.  Work on
state-dependent processor sharing and workload-dependent admission control
studies service rates that depend on the number or composition of active jobs
\citep{gupta2022state,bekker2006admission,altman2006dps}.  The Scheduleurm
co-location curves are a systems instance of the same phenomenon.

Learning-while-scheduling work, including MaxWeight with discounted upper
confidence bound (UCB) and learning-based queueing controls, studies unknown
service statistics under online exploration
\citep{yang2023maxweight,liu2020rlqn,dai2022deep}.  We do not claim a complete
online learning theorem for the current Scheduleurm sampler.  The formal
artifact proves conditional high-probability lifting for active-bucket
certificates.  The current experiment artifact closes the deterministic finite
active-bucket union bound and replay dwell/switching accounting, and adds a
concrete deployable probability model: deterministic round-robin forced
exploration over the finite active bucket set and a bounded two-window
mean-shift detector.  The certificate covers 64 active buckets and 120 replay
scenarios with minimum forced samples 1561 per bucket, Hoeffding radius 0.0523,
detector union tail bound 0.00564, and detection-delay bound 1024 decisions.  A
2026-06-12 optional live-state probe further verifies that the theorem-facing
live hook emits active-bucket sampler and regime-detector records while
preserving the lower-service theorem oracle.  This remains an opt-in extension
path; the default live scheduler policy is not changed.

\subsection{Finite configuration trajectories in operations research}
\label{subsec:related-trajectories}

Several classical operations-research scheduling domains share the same finite
configuration-trajectory structure without sharing the same physical service
model.  In the flexible job-shop scheduling problem (FJSP), a trajectory chooses
an eligible machine and a position in the machine sequence for every operation;
the Kacem instances provide heterogeneous processing times for these choices
\citep{kacem2002pareto}.  In the multi-mode resource-constrained project
scheduling problem (MMRCPSP), each activity chooses a duration/resource mode
subject to precedence and renewable and nonrenewable resource constraints; the
Project Scheduling Problem Library (PSPLIB) supplies standard instances
\citep{kolisch1997psplib}.  In port scheduling, berth positions, specific quay
cranes, crane travel, and setup times form another configuration trajectory;
the Berth Allocation and Specific Quay Crane Assignment Problem with travel and
setup times (BACASP-S) formulation and instances make those costs explicit
\citep{correcher2024berth}.  Section~\ref{subsec:or-generalization} uses these
domains only to test portability of the finite-action selector.  It does not
identify their arrival, service, or reconfiguration laws with those of a GPU
cluster, and it does not transfer the server recurrence theorem without a
domain-specific stochastic model.

\subsection{Configuration actions}
\label{subsec:configuration-actions}

Let \(\mathcal I\) be a finite set of job classes.  Queue \(Q_i(t)\in\mathbb Z_+\)
counts the amount of unfinished work of class \(i\) at the beginning of slot
\(t\).  A global configuration action \(a\in\Afull\) specifies which jobs are
served, which resource configuration each job receives, and where each job is
placed.  In a GPU cluster this includes, for example, the number of jobs sharing
a GPU, CPU worker counts, memory pressure buckets, and host or device placement.
The model intentionally treats these as one action rather than as independent
per-job choices.

Given a regime \(z\) and exogenous randomness \(\omega\), action \(a\) generates
a nonnegative potential-service vector
\(S(a,z,\omega)\in\Real_+^{|\mathcal I|}\).  Potential service is the work that
the configuration could supply under backlog; it may be wasted when a queue is
empty.  We write
\(\mu^z(a)=\E[S(a,z,\omega)]\).  For the main theorem we first suppress \(z\)
and use \(\mu(a)\); uniform-in-regime and average-regime extensions require
additional switching assumptions and are not used as main empirical claims.

Let \(\mathcal F_t\) be the pre-decision filtration generated by the history
through slot \(t\), including \(Q(t)\) and scheduler-visible state \(\chi(t)\).
The feasible families, service tables, penalties, and selected action \(a_t\)
are \(\mathcal F_t\)-measurable; arrivals and potential service are realized
after the action is selected.  Completed departure is
\(D_i(t)=\min\{Q_i(t),S_i(t)\}\), and the queue dynamics are equivalently
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  Q_i(t+1)
  &=Q_i(t)-D_i(t)+A_i(t)\\
  &=\left[Q_i(t)-S_i(t)\right]^+ + A_i(t),
  \qquad i\in\mathcal I.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:queue-dynamics}
\end{equation}
Here \(A_i(t)\) is the class-\(i\) arrival in slot \(t\).  In the finite-support
specialization used by the formal proof, the conditional distribution of
\((A(t),S(t))\) depends on \(Q(t)\) through a finite sample space.  In the more
general theorem it is enough to impose the conditional mean and second-moment
bounds below with respect to \(\mathcal F_t\).

\subsection{Capacity and support functions}
\label{subsec:capacity-support}

For a finite action family \(\mathcal A\), define the service region
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal C(\mathcal A)
  =
  \left\{\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}x_a\mu(a):x\in\operatorname{Mix}(\mathcal A)\right\},
\end{equation}
and its downward closure
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda(\mathcal A)
  =
  \left\{ \lambda\in\Real_+^{|\mathcal I|}:
    \exists v\in\mathcal C(\mathcal A)\ \text{with}\ \lambda\le v
  \right\}.
\end{equation}
The support function in the nonnegative orthant is
\begin{equation}
  H_{\mathcal A}(q)=\max_{a\in\mathcal A}q^\top \mu(a),
  \qquad q\in\Real_+^{|\mathcal I|}.
\end{equation}
Here \(\operatorname{Mix}(\mathcal A)\) is the probability simplex over the action family
\(\mathcal A\), and \(x_a\) is the stationary mixing mass assigned to action
\(a\).  The vector \(q\) is a nonnegative support weight; it is not the queue
state unless explicitly specialized to \(Q(t)\).
If \(\lambda\) has coordinate slack \(\delta>0\) in the full region, then there
is a stationary mixture \(x\in\operatorname{Mix}(\Afull)\) such that
\(\lambda_i+\delta\le \sum_a x_a\mu_i(a)\) for every \(i\).  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
  q^\top\lambda+\delta\normone{q}\le H_{\Afull}(q),
  \qquad q\ge0.
  \label{eq:support-slack}
\end{equation}
Inequality~\eqref{eq:support-slack} is the bridge from capacity to MaxWeight
drift; it is not an operational stability ``if and only if'' by itself.

\subsection{Candidate families and fabric metrics}
\label{subsec:candidate-cover}

The implemented scheduler optimizes over \(\Acand\subseteq\Afull\).  To avoid
turning this computational restriction into an unaccounted model change, define
a finite feature map \(\Phi:\Afull\to\Real^m\) and weighted \(\ell_1\) fabric metric
\begin{equation}
  d_{\Phi}(a,a')=\sum_{r=1}^m w_r|\Phi_r(a)-\Phi_r(a')|.
\end{equation}
The features may include co-location profile, node bucket, CPU bucket, memory
pressure bucket, placement locality, and other scheduler-visible fabric
attributes.  We say that \(\Acand\) is a \(\rho\)-cover of \(\Afull\) if for each
full action \(a\) there is a candidate action \(\pi(a)\in\Acand\) with
\(d_{\Phi}(a,\pi(a))\le\rho\).  If
\(\|\mu(a)-\mu(a')\|_{\infty}\le L d_{\Phi}(a,a')\), then
\begin{equation}
  H_{\Afull}(q)\le H_{\Acand}(q)+L\rho\normone{q},
  \qquad q\ge0.
  \label{eq:cover-support-loss}
\end{equation}
The support-loss bound~\eqref{eq:cover-support-loss} is used as a theorem
assumption unless a perturbation-profiling table certifies \(\Phi,L,\rho\).  For
exact measured finite slices in which the candidate family enumerates the
measured actions, we set \(\rho=0\).

The next result makes the candidate construction explicit rather than taking a
covering family as a primitive.  Let \(\mathcal J_\Phi\) be a finite collection
of active feature cells and let
\(\kappa:\Afull\to\mathcal J_\Phi\) assign each full action to a cell.  For every
active cell, choose one full-action representative
\(\operatorname{rep}(j)\), and define
\begin{equation}
  \Acand
  =
  \left\{
    \operatorname{rep}(j):j\in\mathcal J_\Phi
  \right\}.
  \label{eq:cell-candidate-family}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-cell candidate construction]
\label{prop:finite-cell-candidate}
Suppose every \(a\in\Afull\) and its representative satisfy
\begin{equation}
  d_\Phi\!\left(a,\operatorname{rep}(\kappa(a))\right)\le\rho,
  \label{eq:cell-radius}
\end{equation}
and the service map is \(L\)-Lipschitz in \(d_\Phi\).  Then the family in
\eqref{eq:cell-candidate-family} satisfies
\begin{equation}
  |\Acand|\le |\mathcal J_\Phi|
  \label{eq:candidate-cardinality}
\end{equation}
and, for every \(q\ge0\),
\begin{equation}
  H_{\Afull}(q)
  \le
  H_{\Acand}(q)+L\rho\normone{q}.
  \label{eq:constructed-cover-support-loss}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\proof{Proof.}
The candidate family is the image of the active-cell set under
\(\operatorname{rep}\), so its cardinality cannot exceed
\(|\mathcal J_\Phi|\).  For each \(a\in\Afull\), let
\(a'=\operatorname{rep}(\kappa(a))\).  By~\eqref{eq:cell-radius} and
Lipschitzness,
\(\norminf{\mu(a)-\mu(a')}\le L\rho\).  Nonnegativity of \(q\) gives
\begin{equation}
  q^\top\mu(a)
  \le q^\top\mu(a')+L\rho\normone{q}.
\end{equation}
Taking the maximum over \(a\in\Afull\) proves
\eqref{eq:constructed-cover-support-loss}.
\endproof

Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-cell-candidate} separates two obligations.  The
feature partition and representative map are constructive algorithmic objects;
the radius and service sensitivity remain calibration objects.  Exact measured
slices are the special case in which every active cell is represented exactly
and \(\rho=0\).  A nonzero empirical \(L\rho\) claim additionally requires the
perturbation profiling described in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\section{Robust Candidate MaxWeight}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Policy}
\label{subsec:policy}

Let \(\lowermu_t(a)\) be a conservative lower-service estimate for candidate
action \(a\) at time \(t\).  Let \(K_t(a)=K(a_{t-1},a)\) model switching or
rollback cost from the previous action to \(a\), and let \(G_t(a)\) model risk
or interference penalties.  The exact robust candidate MaxWeight rule is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  a_t\in\arg\max_{a\in\Acand_t}
  \bigl\{&
    Q(t)^\top\lowermu_t(a)\\
    &{}-K_t(a)-G_t(a)
  \bigr\}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:robust-policy}
\end{equation}
For practical schedulers, the selected action may be only approximately optimal.
We assume the queue-scaled oracle condition relative to~\eqref{eq:robust-policy},
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  &\max_{a\in\Acand_t}
  \left\{Q^\top\lowermu_t(a)-K_t(a)-G_t(a)\right\}\\
  &\quad{}-
  \left\{Q^\top\lowermu_t(a_t)-K_t(a_t)-G_t(a_t)\right\}\\
  &\le \alpha_0+\alpha_1\normone{Q}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:oracle-error}
\end{equation}
The oracle loss in~\eqref{eq:oracle-error} is paid from the same slack budget as
candidate loss and lower-service error.  Exact maximization is the special case
\(\alpha_0=\alpha_1=0\).

\begin{assumption}[Load slack]
\label{ass:load-slack}
The arrival mean vector \(\lambda\) has full-action support slack
\(\delta>0\):
\begin{equation}
  q^\top\lambda+\delta\normone{q}\le H_{\Afull}(q),
  \qquad q\in\Real_+^{|\mathcal I|}.
\end{equation}
This is the support-function form of belonging to the interior of the
full-action capacity base.  It is a sufficient stability hypothesis; the
operational necessity direction requires a separate conservation law.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Candidate cover and lower-service error]
\label{ass:candidate-lower-service}
For every scheduler-visible state \(t\) and every
\(q\in\Real_+^{|\mathcal I|}\),
\begin{equation}
  H_{\Afull}(q)\le
  H_{\Acand_t}(q)+\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}\normone{q},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  H_{\Acand_t}(q)\le
  \max_{a\in\Acand_t}q^\top\lowermu_t(a)
  +\epsilon_{\mathrm{est}}\normone{q}.
\end{equation}
When the fabric metric in Section~\ref{subsec:candidate-cover} is certified,
\(\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}=L\rho\).  For an exact measured finite slice,
\(\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}=0\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Diagonal-scaled lower-confidence support loss]
\label{prop:diagonal-lcb}
Let \(\widehat\mu_i(a)\) estimate \(\mu_i(a)\), let \(r_i(a)\) be a
coordinate confidence radius, and let \(s_i\ge0\) be a fixed service-unit
scale.  On the simultaneous confidence event
\begin{equation}
  \left|\widehat\mu_i(a)-\mu_i(a)\right|
  \le r_i(a),
  \qquad
  0\le r_i(a)\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{LCB}}s_i,
  \label{eq:diagonal-confidence-event}
\end{equation}
for every admitted \(a\in\Acand_t\) and class \(i\), define
\begin{equation}
  \lowermu_{t,i}(a)=\widehat\mu_i(a)-r_i(a).
  \label{eq:diagonal-lcb-service}
\end{equation}
Then \(\lowermu_{t,i}(a)\le\mu_i(a)\) and, for every \(q\ge0\),
\begin{equation}
  H_{\Acand_t}(q)
  \le
  \max_{a\in\Acand_t}q^\top\lowermu_t(a)
  +2\epsilon_{\mathrm{LCB}}\sum_i s_iq_i.
  \label{eq:diagonal-lcb-support}
\end{equation}
If \(s_i\le C_s\) for every \(i\), Assumption~\ref{ass:candidate-lower-service}
holds with
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon_{\mathrm{est}}=2\epsilon_{\mathrm{LCB}}C_s.
  \label{eq:diagonal-estimation-loss}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\proof{Proof.}
The upper side of~\eqref{eq:diagonal-confidence-event} gives
\(\widehat\mu_i(a)-r_i(a)\le\mu_i(a)\).  Its lower side and the radius bound
give
\begin{equation}
  \mu_i(a)
  \le
  \widehat\mu_i(a)+r_i(a)
  \le
  \lowermu_{t,i}(a)+2\epsilon_{\mathrm{LCB}}s_i.
\end{equation}
Multiplying by \(q_i\ge0\), summing over \(i\), and maximizing over admitted
actions proves~\eqref{eq:diagonal-lcb-support}.  Finally,
\(\sum_i s_iq_i\le C_s\sum_iq_i=C_s\normone{q}\), which yields
\eqref{eq:diagonal-estimation-loss}.
\endproof

\begin{assumption}[Penalty growth and approximate oracle]
\label{ass:penalty-oracle}
Let \(P_t(a)=K_t(a)+G_t(a)\).  For all \(a\in\Acand_t\),
\begin{equation}
  0\le P_t(a)\le P_0+\beta\normone{Q(t)}.
\end{equation}
The selected action \(a_t\in\Acand_t\) satisfies the approximate-oracle
condition
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  &\max_{a\in\Acand_t}
  \left\{Q(t)^\top\lowermu_t(a)-P_t(a)\right\}\\
  &\quad{}-
  \left\{Q(t)^\top\lowermu_t(a_t)-P_t(a_t)\right\}
  \le \alpha_0+\alpha_1\normone{Q(t)}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Exact robust MaxWeight is the special case
\(\alpha_0=\alpha_1=0\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Stochastic domination and bounded second moments]
\label{ass:stochastic-moments}
The action \(a_t\), statewise action families, lower-service map, and penalties
are \(\mathcal F_t\)-measurable.  For every class \(i\),
\begin{equation}
  \E[A_i(t)\mid\mathcal F_t]\le\lambda_i,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  \lowermu_{t,i}(a_t)
  \le \E[S_i(t)\mid\mathcal F_t]
\end{equation}
almost surely.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
  \E\!\left[
    \frac12\sum_i\{A_i(t)^2+S_i(t)^2\}
    \,\middle|\,\mathcal F_t
  \right]
  \le B.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Countable-state recurrence condition]
\label{ass:foster-state}
For a positive-recurrence conclusion, the augmented controlled state
\(X(t)=(Q(t),\chi(t))\) is a Markov chain on a countable state space.  The set
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal C_N
  =\{x:\normone{Q(x)}\le N\}
\end{equation}
used by the drift certificate is finite and nonempty, and the corresponding
closed class satisfies the usual local-return condition.  Without these
conditions we claim only the finite-set negative-drift certificate.
\end{assumption}

\subsubsection{Deterministic slack accounting}
\label{subsubsec:deterministic-slack}

\begin{lemma}[Support slack after candidate and estimation losses]
\label{lem:support-after-losses}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:load-slack}--\ref{ass:candidate-lower-service}, for
every \(t\) and \(q\in\Real_+^{|\mathcal I|}\),
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \max_{a\in\Acand_t} q^\top\lowermu_t(a)
  &\ge q^\top\lambda\\
  &\quad{}
  +(\delta-\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}
  -\epsilon_{\mathrm{est}})\normone{q}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\proof{Proof.}
By Assumption~\ref{ass:load-slack},
\begin{equation}
q^\top\lambda+\delta\normone{q}\le H_{\Afull}(q).
\end{equation}
Assumption~\ref{ass:candidate-lower-service} gives
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  H_{\Afull}(q)
  &\le H_{\Acand_t}(q)
      +\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}\normone{q}\\
  &\le
  \max_{a\in\Acand_t}q^\top\lowermu_t(a)\\
  &\quad{}+
  (\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{est}})\normone{q}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Rearranging proves the claim.
\endproof

\begin{lemma}[Selected lower-service margin]
\label{lem:selected-margin}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:load-slack}--\ref{ass:penalty-oracle}, for
\(Q=Q(t)\),
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  Q^\top\lowermu_t(a_t)
  &\ge Q^\top\lambda\\
  &\quad{}+
  \bigl(\delta-\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}
  -\epsilon_{\mathrm{est}}
  -\beta-\alpha_1\bigr)\normone{Q}\\
  &\quad{}-(P_0+\alpha_0).
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\proof{Proof.}
Let \(M(Q)=\max_{a\in\Acand_t}Q^\top\lowermu_t(a)\).  Since
\(P_t(a)\le P_0+\beta\normone{Q}\) for every candidate,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  &\max_{a\in\Acand_t}
  \{Q^\top\lowermu_t(a)-P_t(a)\}\\
  &\quad{}\ge M(Q)-P_0-\beta\normone{Q}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The approximate-oracle condition therefore implies
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  Q^\top\lowermu_t(a_t)-P_t(a_t)
  &\ge M(Q)-P_0-\beta\normone{Q}\\
  &\quad{}-\alpha_0-\alpha_1\normone{Q}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Because \(P_t(a_t)\ge0\), the left-hand side is at most
\(Q^\top\lowermu_t(a_t)\).  Applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:support-after-losses} with \(q=Q\) gives the stated inequality.
\endproof

\begin{lemma}[Quadratic one-step bound]
\label{lem:quadratic-step}
For \(V(Q)=\frac12\sum_iQ_i^2\) and nonnegative vectors \(Q,A,S\),
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  &V([Q-S]^+ + A)-V(Q)\\
  &\quad\le
  \frac12\sum_i(A_i^2+S_i^2)
  +Q^\top A-Q^\top S.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\proof{Proof.}
It is enough to prove the coordinate inequality.  For \(q,a,s\ge0\),
\(([q-s]^+)^2\le(q-s)^2\) and \([q-s]^+\le q\).  Hence
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  ([q-s]^++a)^2
  &\le ([q-s]^+)^2+a^2+2a[q-s]^+\\
  &\le q^2+s^2+a^2+2q(a-s).
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Subtracting \(q^2\), dividing by two, and summing over coordinates gives the
result.
\endproof

\begin{theorem}[Robust candidate MaxWeight stability certificate]
\label{thm:main}
Consider a finite system with job classes \(\mathcal I\) and queue
dynamics~\eqref{eq:queue-dynamics}.  Suppose
Assumptions~\ref{ass:load-slack}--\ref{ass:stochastic-moments} hold.  If
\begin{equation}
  \eta=\delta-(\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{est}}+\beta+\alpha_1)>0,
\end{equation}
then for \(V(Q)=\frac12\sum_i Q_i^2\) and
\(\Delta V(t)=V(Q(t+1))-V(Q(t))\),
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \E[\Delta V(t)\mid\mathcal F_t]
  &\le B+P_0+\alpha_0\\
  &\quad{}-\eta\normone{Q(t)}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for any \(N\) satisfying
\begin{equation}
  B+P_0+\alpha_0+\gamma \le \eta N
\end{equation}
for some \(\gamma>0\), the process has a negative expected drift outside
\(\mathcal C_N\).  If Assumption~\ref{ass:foster-state} also holds,
the standard countable-state Foster criterion gives positive recurrence of the
corresponding closed communicating class.
\end{theorem}

\proof{Proof.}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:quadratic-step} with
\((Q,A,S)=(Q(t),A(t),S(t))\) and condition on \(\mathcal F_t\).  Using
Assumption~\ref{ass:stochastic-moments},
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \E[\Delta V(t)\mid\mathcal F_t]
  &\le B
  +Q(t)^\top\E[A(t)\mid\mathcal F_t]\\
  &\quad{}-Q(t)^\top\E[S(t)\mid\mathcal F_t]\\
  &\le B+Q(t)^\top\lambda
       -Q(t)^\top\lowermu_t(a_t).
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Write
\(D_t=Q(t)^\top\lambda-Q(t)^\top\lowermu_t(a_t)\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:selected-margin} gives
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  D_t&\le P_0+\alpha_0\\
  &\quad{}-
  \bigl(\delta-\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}
  -\epsilon_{\mathrm{est}}
  -\beta-\alpha_1\bigr)\normone{Q(t)}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Combining the two displays proves
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \E[\Delta V(t)\mid\mathcal F_t]
  &\le B+P_0+\alpha_0\\
  &\quad{}-\eta\normone{Q(t)}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
If \(B+P_0+\alpha_0+\gamma\le\eta N\), the drift is at most
\(-\gamma\) whenever \(\normone{Q(t)}>N\).  This is the finite-set Foster drift
certificate.  Under Assumption~\ref{ass:foster-state}, the usual
countable-state Foster theorem yields positive recurrence of the closed class.
\endproof

\begin{corollary}[Finite-horizon expected-backlog bound]
\label{cor:finite-horizon-backlog}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, suppose the Lyapunov variables
are integrable.  For every integer \(T\ge1\),
\begin{equation}
  \frac1T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
  \E\!\left[\normone{Q(t)}\right]
  \le
  \frac{B+P_0+\alpha_0}{\eta}
  +\frac{\E[V(Q(0))]}{\eta T}.
  \label{eq:finite-horizon-backlog}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
  \limsup_{T\to\infty}
  \frac1T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
  \E\!\left[\normone{Q(t)}\right]
  \le
  \frac{B+P_0+\alpha_0}{\eta}.
  \label{eq:asymptotic-backlog}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\proof{Proof.}
Take expectations in the conditional drift bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, sum from \(t=0\) to \(T-1\), and apply the tower
property.  The Lyapunov terms telescope and \(V\ge0\), so
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \eta\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\E[\normone{Q(t)}]
  &\le T(B+P_0+\alpha_0)\\
  &\quad{}+\E[V(Q(0))].
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Dividing by \(\eta T\) gives
\eqref{eq:finite-horizon-backlog}; taking \(T\to\infty\) gives
\eqref{eq:asymptotic-backlog}.
\endproof

\begin{corollary}[Statewise feasible families]
\label{cor:statewise}
Let the full family, candidate family, true service, lower service, feature map,
and penalty be indexed by the scheduler-visible state
\(X(t)=(Q(t),\chi(t))\):
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \Afull_t&=\Afull(X(t)),
  &\Acand_t&=\Acand(X(t)),\\
  \mu_t&=\mu(X(t),\cdot),
  &\lowermu_t&=\lowermu(X(t),\cdot),\\
  \Phi_t&=\Phi(X(t),\cdot),
  &P_t&=P(X(t),\cdot).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:statewise-objects}
\end{equation}
Here \(\chi(t)\) records scheduler-visible availability, pinned jobs, node
health, and measured capacity boundaries.  Suppose the load-slack, calibrated
candidate-cover, lower-service, penalty, oracle, and stochastic-moment
conditions hold pointwise for every admitted state \(X(t)\), with the same
constants
\(\delta,\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{est}},P_0,\beta,\alpha_0,
\alpha_1,B\).  Then the drift bound and the finite-horizon backlog bounds in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-horizon-backlog} hold
unchanged.  If Assumption~\ref{ass:foster-state} holds for the augmented state,
the same finite-set recurrence conclusion follows.
\end{corollary}

\proof{Proof.}
The proof of Lemmas~\ref{lem:support-after-losses}--\ref{lem:selected-margin}
is pointwise in all six objects in~\eqref{eq:statewise-objects}.  Conditioning
on \(\mathcal F_t\) fixes the realized full and candidate families, service
maps, features, penalties, and action.  The same inequality chain therefore
gives the same lower-service margin and quadratic drift bound.  Uniformity of
the constants over the admitted statewise family permits the tower-property
and Foster steps without introducing an additional state-dependent loss.
\endproof

The Lean 4 theorem prover artifact \citep{deMoura2021lean} proves the

form above is the conventional augmented-state extension; its recurrence

\subsection{Proof decomposition}
\label{subsec:proof-decomposition}

The preceding lemmas isolate the mathematical obligations that must be certified
by the scheduler.  Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-cell-candidate} constructs a
bounded candidate family; Proposition~\ref{prop:diagonal-lcb} converts
coordinate confidence radii into a support loss;
Lemma~\ref{lem:support-after-losses} is the capacity and approximation layer;
Lemma~\ref{lem:selected-margin} is the robust oracle layer;
Lemma~\ref{lem:quadratic-step} is the queueing layer; and
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with
Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-horizon-backlog} is their Foster-Lyapunov composition.
The Lean formalization mirrors this separation.  The operational necessity direction is
kept separate: an ``operationally stabilizes'' predicate is load-certified by a
model that explicitly encodes the mean arrival vector, and zero-slack necessity
follows from a conservation-law argument rather than from the definition of the
predicate.

\section{Scheduleurm Instantiation}
\label{sec:implementation}

\subsection{Measured action slices}
\label{subsec:measured-slices}

Scheduleurm is an existing scheduler for local and remote research jobs.  The
new algorithmic layer deliberately separates the theorem-facing policy from the
legacy launcher.  The theorem-facing policy builds global or statewise candidate
families with a queue vector, lower-service vector, penalty units, and robust
MaxWeight score.  Service profiling, replay, baseline comparison, slack
accounting, and oracle-gap checks are implemented outside the legacy scheduler.

global-batch soft-hint path.  The global-batch hook computes a bounded candidate
configuration and returns only a preferred-node hint to the legacy launcher.
Final placement therefore still passes through the existing resource and launch
checks.  This design lets us test the theorem-facing policy without making it
the default production dispatcher.

The replay policy uses a queue-adaptive robust MaxWeight penalty selector rather
than a fixed co-location profile.  For a workload with backlog count \(b\), it
first restricts profiles to a measured support envelope \(\mathcal K_i\) and
then chooses
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  k_i^\star(b)
  &\in
  \argmax_{k\in\mathcal K_i}
  \left\{
  b\,\widehat\mu_i(k)
  -\theta_{\mathrm{prof}}\widehat\mu_i^{\max}k
  \right\},\\
  \theta_{\mathrm{prof}}&=0.10 .
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathcal K_i\) is the measured support envelope for class \(i\),
\(\widehat\mu_i(k)\) is the measured aggregate service for class \(i\) at
profile \(k\), and \(\widehat\mu_i^{\max}\) is the best measured aggregate
service in the same envelope.  Thus the replay candidate uses lower-interference
profiles such as q01 profile 1 when they stay within the measured support slack,
while support certificates retain actions such as q01 profiles 4 and 8 for
capacity accounting.  The optional statewise drain is lower-service guarded and
no-upshift: it may only reduce a profile when the measured lower-service and
waiting-backlog checks certify no loss relative to the base action.  This
implementation matches the approximate-oracle theorem:
the support envelope preserves the drift action at large \(Q\), and the
finite-batch delay preference is a bounded profile penalty that is paid from the
same slack budget as \(K_t+G_t\).

Scheduleurm distinguishes three populations.  The explicit task-list replay

\subsection{Policy-semantics replay baselines}
\label{subsec:policy-semantics-baselines}

No single external scheduler covers all four Scheduleurm quadrants and the
mixed portfolio without changing the workload model.  For this reason the
external-scheduler evidence is organized as a diagnostic layer rather than as
the central claim of the paper.  We compare against policy-semantics replay
baselines on the same measured service cache: a throughput-table goodput policy
representing Gavel, Pollux, and Sia-like schedulers
\citep{narayanan2020gavel,qiao2021pollux,sia2023}; a finite-batch delay oracle
representing shortest remaining processing time (SRPT), Gittins, and shortest
expected remaining processing time (SERPT)-style policies
\citep{scully2020simple}; and an interference guard representing
IADeep/Salus/Gandiva-like co-location control
\citep{xiao2018gandiva,yu2020salus,iadeep2023}.  Because every row is evaluated through
the same measured lower-service map, these baselines test whether the finite
candidate family is missing an obvious scheduling semantic; they are not direct
binary executions of the external systems.

We also register 11 non-adjacent scheduler families as finite actions in the
Scheduleurm+external action union.  This is the form needed by the
candidate-set theorem: once an external policy family is represented by a
lower-service row and a bounded penalty, it can be evaluated by the same robust
selector.  Several systems also have scoped same-host runtime checks, and
Decima has a same-domain Spark directed acyclic graph (DAG) simulator check.
These checks support traceability.  They are not used as the central comparison
because the paper's optimization claim is about measured configuration actions,
not about deploying every external control plane under identical production
conditions.

The multi-node check covers Scheduleurm's own measured history.  Two GPU nodes
were reachable during the read-only fabric probe.  The resulting candidate set
contained 18 lower-service rows and 626 disjoint robust-MaxWeight
configurations, including a selected two-node configuration with
\(\alpha_0=\alpha_1=0\).  A separate scheduler-history study found 4,058
strict theorem-admitted launches, 4,047 completions, 35 workload domains, 13
nodes, three GPU nodes, and no strict unadmitted launched rows.  This supports a
Scheduleurm-native multi-node history claim.  It does not require claiming that
each external scheduler has been run through its original multi-node control
plane.

the measured action slice, so \(L\rho=0\).  The resulting certificate is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \delta &= 0.081934686,\\
  \epsilon_{\mathrm{est}} &= \beta=\alpha_1=0,\\
  \eta &= 0.081934686.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The finite-support second-moment bound is \(B=11209514.9680047\), and the resulting

fabric-cover metric has already been calibrated with \(L\rho=0\), or that
holdout lower-service error vanishes outside the measured rows.

\subsection{Finite-trajectory generalization beyond compute}
\label{subsec:or-generalization}

The server action can be generalized without erasing domain physics by treating
an action as a complete finite configuration trajectory.  A trajectory records
placement or mode choices, admissible reconfiguration decisions, cumulative
lower service, duration, and a bounded penalty.  In each experiment below, the
same selector exactly maximizes generated-family robust score
\begin{equation}
  a_n\in\argmax_{a\in\mathcal A_n^{\mathrm{traj}}}
  \left\{
    \frac{
      \left(Q^{\mathrm F}(n)\right)^{\!\top}
      \underline S_n^{\mathrm F}(a)-P_n^{\mathrm F}(a)}
    {\tau_n(a)}
  \right\},
  \label{eq:trajectory-selector}
\end{equation}
Here \(n\) indexes decision frames, \(Q^{\mathrm F}(n)\) is the frame-boundary
backlog or urgency vector, \(\mathcal A_n^{\mathrm{traj}}\) is the finite
generated trajectory family, \(\underline S_n^{\mathrm F}(a)\) is the
cumulative lower-service vector, \(P_n^{\mathrm F}(a)\) is the cumulative
bounded trajectory penalty, and \(\tau_n(a)>0\) is the trajectory duration.  Dividing
the denominator by a positive horizon common to every action in the frame
does not change the argmax; this is the normalized score used by the artifact.
The duration factor is essential: maximizing the unnormalized cumulative
numerator is generally not equivalent when candidate trajectories have
different lengths.  The queue/urgency coordinates and feasibility oracle are
domain-specific.  Proposition~\ref{prop:frame-drift} supplies the
variable-duration stochastic interface, and the Lean theorem
\texttt{frameApproximateOracle\_iff\_durationNormalized} checks the corresponding
cumulative-oracle/rate-oracle equivalence for positive duration.  The
deterministic holdouts in this subsection test the candidate/oracle/penalty
layer and do not by themselves instantiate a stochastic arrival model.

The latest confirmation protocols augment, rather than overwrite, the earlier
feasibility holdouts.  The FJSP confirmation selects 20 previously unused
instances from the Hurink edata, rdata, sdata, and vdata families
\citep{hurink1994tabu}.  For each instance, the robust selector and five fixed
dispatch rules are compared using exact integer makespan and sum-completion
ledgers.  The fixed rules are first-in-first-out (FIFO), earliest completion
time (ECT), shortest processing time (SPT), most operations remaining (MOR), and
most work remaining (MWKR).  A separate five-second, single-worker OR-Tools
9.14 Constraint Programming with Boolean Satisfiability (CP-SAT) run provides a
makespan reference \citep{perron2023cpsat}.  Its sum-completion coordinate is
descriptive because CP-SAT optimizes makespan only, and a feasible status is not
an optimality proof.

The MMRCPSP experiment registers 25 feasible PSPLIB trajectories, five each
from j12, j14, j16, j18, and j20 \citep{kolisch1997psplib}.  One action completes
one project from one registered class.  Seven exogenous unit-time arrival
scenarios are replayed against duration-normalized robust MaxWeight,
unnormalized robust MaxWeight, shortest registered trajectory, longest queue
then shortest trajectory, and round-robin class selection.  The first stream
matrix uses three seeds; a later class-balance protocol freezes five new arrival
tapes.  The class-balance metric family was registered after observing the
first matrix but before observing those new tapes.  It is therefore a
prospective arrival-tape check on a known instance and action library, not a new
structural-instance holdout.

The port evidence keeps two models non-substitutable.  The BACASP-S source-core

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

This paper connects a queueing-stability theorem with the objects available in
a real heterogeneous scheduler.  The main mathematical point is that candidate
restriction is not treated as a heuristic shortcut.  It is represented as an
explicit support-function loss and paid from capacity slack together with
estimation, penalty, and oracle losses.  This gives a readable accounting rule:
a scheduler may use a finite candidate family only when the remaining drift
margin is still positive.

The finite-cell construction strengthens this accounting from a purely
assumption-driven statement to a candidate-generation result.  A finite feature
partition controls candidate cardinality, while representative radius and
service sensitivity control the induced support loss.  This separates
computational complexity from statistical calibration: the partition determines
how many actions the oracle must inspect, whereas perturbation data determine
how much capacity slack the compression consumes.  The diagonal LCB result
performs the analogous separation across heterogeneous service units, and the
finite-horizon corollary converts residual slack into an expected-backlog bound
rather than only a recurrence statement.

The Scheduleurm case study shows how this accounting can be implemented.  The
scheduler records measured lower-service rows, admissible configuration
actions, capacity boundaries, penalties, oracle gaps, and admission status.  The
same records support four roles: performance replay, stability-condition
calibration, production-history classification, and optional live-hook checks.
This separation is useful because it prevents a strong empirical result in one
population from being silently transferred to another.

The strongest empirical statement remains a measured finite-slice statement.
For the declared service domains, exact measured actions give \(\rho=0\), and
compressed candidate families report the corresponding nonzero \(L\rho\).  A
future task is theorem-facing only if it is admitted to a measured domain with a
positive lower-service row; otherwise it is assigned to a probe/defer state with
zero theorem service.  This is sufficient for the present measured-domain
certificate, but it is not a universal positive-service theorem for all future
workloads and hardware states.

The external-scheduler comparisons have a similarly precise role.  They test
whether the measured candidate family omits a useful scheduling semantic, such
as goodput allocation, delay priority, or interference guarding.  Once such a
semantic is represented by a lower-service row and a bounded penalty, it becomes
another finite action in the robust selector.  This is the right comparison for
the candidate-set theorem.  It is narrower than a claim that Scheduleurm has
beaten every external scheduler in its native full-stack deployment.

The production evidence should also be read at the correct level.  Controlled
live launches show that admitted theorem-hook actions can run to completion.
Strict scheduler history shows that admitted production rows can be classified
and completed at scale.  Read-only shadow traces show that current queue states
can be converted into theorem-facing candidate rows without mutating the queue.
Together these checks support the measured Scheduleurm certificate.  They do
not turn raw telemetry, future unknown arrivals, or every live dispatch into a
stability-certified arrival stream.

Three technical directions remain open.  The first is broader fabric-cover
calibration: extending the positive-service certificate beyond the declared
finite domains requires perturbation evidence for the feature map, projection,
cover radius, Lipschitz envelope, and statistical failure probability.  The
second is online learning: the active-bucket sampler and regime detector have
formal conditional guarantees and replay checks, but they should be validated
in live A/B experiments before becoming default scheduling logic.  The third is
migration-aware control.  Proposition~\ref{prop:frame-drift} closes the
mathematical variable-duration boundary: setup time, lost work, and resume risk
enter a cumulative net-service frame or a unit-matched bounded penalty, and a
positive minimum duration transfers per-time slack to embedded-chain drift.
The remaining obligation is operational rather than algebraic.  The current
controlled migration rows estimate checkpoint, synchronization, staging, and
resume components, but they have not yet been assembled into a domain-wide
stochastic frame model with conditional cumulative-service and moment
certificates.  We therefore do not infer a semi-Markov stability claim from the
deterministic migration or cross-domain trajectory holdouts.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied heterogeneous compute scheduling as a configuration-action
stochastic processing network.  The resulting robust candidate MaxWeight
theorem gives an explicit slack budget for candidate approximation,
lower-service estimation, bounded penalties, and approximate optimization.  If
the residual margin is positive, the policy satisfies finite-horizon
expected-backlog bounds and a finite-set Foster-Lyapunov recurrence certificate.
A finite feature-cell construction bounds both candidate cardinality and the
resulting \(L\rho\) support loss, while diagonal scaling preserves the meaning of
lower-confidence service across heterogeneous workload units.  A separate
variable-duration frame result extends the same slack accounting to cumulative
configuration trajectories and recovers the slotted theorem at unit duration.
The fixed-family, queue-indexed statewise, candidate-construction,
diagonal-scaling, and frame statements are formalized in Lean.

Scheduleurm provides a concrete instantiation of the theorem.  Its measured
finite slices close the lower-service stability conditions for declared
workload domains.  On explicit task lists and 192 online replay scenarios, the
robust candidate improves legacy completion metrics and is not Pareto-dominated
by registered policy-semantics baselines evaluated on the same measured service
cache.  Controlled live launches and strict scheduler-history evidence show how
the theorem-facing objects can be produced by an operational scheduler.  Frozen
finite-trajectory experiments on FJSP, MMRCPSP, and port models further test the
candidate/oracle/penalty interface.  Their mixed results include
independent-solver and registered-policy counterexamples, so they support
portability of the interface without claiming domain-wide optimality or
automatic transfer of the server stochastic model.

The contribution is therefore not a universal claim about every future workload
or every external scheduler implementation.  It is a mathematical and empirical
template for making approximate configuration-action scheduling certifiable:
define the action family, measure conservative service, account for every loss,
and claim stability only when the residual slack remains positive.

\section*{Appendix: Conventional Proof Details}
\label{app:conventional-proof}

This appendix gives the conventional, paper-readable proof chain behind
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  It is intentionally redundant with the main text:
the purpose is to make clear which part is mathematics, which part is a
measured Scheduleurm certificate, and which part is checked by Lean.  The Lean
artifact is a formal check of the algebraic and recurrence implications; it is
not a substitute for empirical certificates of service rows, lower confidence
bounds, candidate admission, oracle gaps, or moment bounds.

\subsection*{EC.1. Capacity Slack as a Support-Function Margin}

Let \(\mu(a)\in\Real_+^{|\mathcal I|}\) denote the true mean service vector of
full action \(a\in\Afull\), and let \(H_{\Afull}(q)=\max_{a\in\Afull}q^\top
\mu(a)\) be the full-action support function for nonnegative backlog weights
\(q\in\Real_+^{|\mathcal I|}\).  A stationary randomized policy over full
actions is a probability vector \(x\in\operatorname{Mix}(\Afull)\).  If it has coordinate
slack \(\delta>0\) against the arrival mean \(\lambda\), then
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_i+\delta \le \sum_{a\in\Afull}x_a \mu_i(a),
  \qquad i\in\mathcal I.
\end{equation}
Multiplying each coordinate by \(q_i\ge0\) and summing gives
\begin{equation}
  q^\top\lambda+\delta\normone{q}
  \le
  \sum_{a\in\Afull}x_a q^\top \mu(a).
\end{equation}
Because \(x\) is a convex combination, the right-hand side is at most the
maximum full-action support:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{a\in\Afull}x_a q^\top \mu(a)
  \le
  H_{\Afull}(q).
\end{equation}
Hence full-action capacity slack implies
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ec-capacity-support}
  q^\top\lambda+\delta\normone{q}\le H_{\Afull}(q).
\end{equation}
This is the only place where the capacity-region hypothesis enters the drift
proof.  It converts an interior capacity statement into a MaxWeight support
margin that can be compared to candidate actions.  The paper uses the
support-function statement as Assumption~\ref{ass:load-slack}; the operational
necessity direction is separate and requires a load-certified stochastic model
and a conservation law.

\subsection*{EC.2. Candidate Fabric Cover and Support Loss}

The scheduler cannot enumerate every full configuration.  It evaluates an
admitted candidate family \(\Acand_t\subseteq\Afull\), possibly depending on
the observed state \(t\).  The finite-candidate theorem needs a support loss
certificate: for every \(q\ge0\),
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ec-candidate-support}
  H_{\Afull}(q)\le H_{\Acand_t}(q)
  +\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}\normone{q}.
\end{equation}
When the loss is obtained from a calibrated fabric metric, let
\(\Phi(a)\) be the finite feature representation of action \(a\), let
\(d_{\Phi}\) be the feature metric, and suppose each full action \(a\) has a projected
candidate \(\pi_t(a)\in\Acand_t\) satisfying
\begin{equation}
  d_{\Phi}(a,\pi_t(a))\le\rho.
\end{equation}
If service is Lipschitz in this representation,
\begin{equation}
  \norminf{\mu(a)-\mu(\pi_t(a))}\le L\,d_{\Phi}(a,\pi_t(a)),
\end{equation}
then for any maximizer \(a^\star\in\argmax_{a\in\Afull}q^\top \mu(a)\),
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  H_{\Afull}(q)
  &=q^\top \mu(a^\star)\\
  &\le q^\top \mu(\pi_t(a^\star))+\normone{q}L\rho\\
  &\le H_{\Acand_t}(q)+L\rho\normone{q}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}=L\rho\) in the fabric-cover case.  In measured
finite slices where the candidate support is certified directly, the artifact
uses the observed support gap itself as \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}\); when the
candidate set is exact on the measured slice, this term is zero.  This is why
the proof does not shrink the action space informally: every restriction must
be paid for by the explicit support-function loss in
\eqref{eq:ec-candidate-support}.

Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-cell-candidate} supplies a constructive version of
this step.  It forms candidates as representatives of active feature cells,
bounds the candidate cardinality by the number of cells, and derives the same
\(L\rho\) support loss.  Thus the theorem can consume either a directly audited
support gap or a finite-cell construction certificate; only the latter supports
a claim of nontrivial action-space compression.

\subsection*{EC.3. Lower-Service Rows, Penalties, and Approximate Oracles}

The robust scheduler optimizes conservative service, not the unknown true mean
service.  For each admitted candidate \(a\in\Acand_t\), the artifact provides a
lower-service vector \(\lowermu_t(a)\).  The lower-service support loss is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ec-lower-service-support}
  H_{\Acand_t}(q)
  \le
  \max_{a\in\Acand_t}q^\top\lowermu_t(a)
  +\epsilon_{\mathrm{est}}\normone{q}.
\end{equation}
The scheduling score is
\begin{equation}
  \Psi_t(a)
  =
  Q^\top\lowermu_t(a)-K_t(a)-G_t(a),
\end{equation}
where \(K_t(a)\) and \(G_t(a)\) are bounded switching, risk, or guard penalties.
Equation~\eqref{eq:ec-lower-service-support} is the lower-confidence-bound
bridge from measured service rows to the drift proof.  The coordinate-scaled
sufficient condition for this bridge is given in
Proposition~\ref{prop:diagonal-lcb}: on the simultaneous confidence event it
charges \(2\epsilon_{\mathrm{LCB}}\sum_i s_iq_i\), or
\(2\epsilon_{\mathrm{LCB}}C_s\normone{q}\) under a uniform scale bound.  This
prevents a high-throughput workload from defining the absolute error unit for
all other classes.  The selected action
\(a_t\) need not be the exact maximizer.  It satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ec-oracle-gap}
  \max_{a\in\Acand_t}\Psi_t(a)-\Psi_t(a_t)
  \le \alpha_0+\alpha_1\normone{Q},
\end{equation}
and all admitted candidate penalties obey
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ec-penalty-bound}
  0\le K_t(a)+G_t(a)\le P_0+\beta\normone{Q},
  \qquad a\in\Acand_t.
\end{equation}
Combining the capacity support bound \eqref{eq:ec-capacity-support}, the two
support-loss bounds \eqref{eq:ec-candidate-support} and
\eqref{eq:ec-lower-service-support}, the oracle gap
\eqref{eq:ec-oracle-gap}, and the penalty bound
\eqref{eq:ec-penalty-bound} yields
the selected lower-service margin.  First,
\begin{equation}
  \max_{a\in\Acand_t}Q^\top\lowermu_t(a)
  \ge
  Q^\top\lambda+
  (\delta-\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{est}})\normone{Q}.
\end{equation}
Second, by the oracle gap and the all-candidate penalty bound,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ec-selected-margin}
  \begin{aligned}
  Q^\top\lowermu_t(a_t)
  &\ge \max_{a\in\Acand_t}Q^\top\lowermu_t(a)\\
  &\quad{}-(P_0+\alpha_0)-(\beta+\alpha_1)\normone{Q}\\
  &\ge Q^\top\lambda+
     \eta\normone{Q}-(P_0+\alpha_0),
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  \eta=\delta-(\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{est}}+\beta+\alpha_1).
\end{equation}
The theorem therefore has a simple accounting rule: candidate loss,
lower-service estimation loss, queue-scaled penalty, and queue-scaled oracle
loss all consume the same capacity slack.  A stability certificate requires
\(\eta>0\).  Additive terms \(P_0\) and \(\alpha_0\) do not change the slope of
the drift; they enlarge the finite set outside which the drift is negative.

\subsection*{EC.4. Quadratic Drift and the Foster Certificate}

The queue update is
\begin{equation}
  Q_i(t+1)=\bigl[Q_i(t)-S_i(t)\bigr]^+ + A_i(t),
\end{equation}
where \(A_i(t)\) is arrival and \(S_i(t)\) is potential service for class \(i\).
Completed departure is
\(D_i(t)=\min\{Q_i(t),S_i(t)\}\).  The displayed update remains valid because
unused potential service is removed by the positive part.
For \(V(Q)=\frac12\sum_i Q_i^2\), the standard one-step inequality gives
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ec-quadratic-step}
  \begin{aligned}
  \Delta V(t)
  &\le
  \frac12\sum_i\bigl(A_i(t)^2+S_i(t)^2\bigr)\\
  &\quad{}+Q(t)^\top A(t)-Q(t)^\top S(t).
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Assumption~\ref{ass:stochastic-moments} supplies the conditional second-order
bound
\begin{equation}
  \E\!\left[
    \frac12\sum_i\bigl(A_i(t)^2+S_i(t)^2\bigr)
    \,\middle|\,\mathcal F_t
  \right]\le B.
\end{equation}
It also ties the stochastic process to the theorem-facing service rows:
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:ec-arrival-domination}
  \E[A(t)\mid\mathcal F_t]&\le\lambda,\\
  \label{eq:ec-service-domination}
  \E[S(t)\mid\mathcal F_t]&\ge\lowermu_t(a_t)
\end{align}
coordinatewise.  Taking conditional expectation in
\eqref{eq:ec-quadratic-step} and using \eqref{eq:ec-selected-margin},
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ec-drift-bound}
  \begin{aligned}
  \E[\Delta V(t)\mid\mathcal F_t]
  &\le
  B+Q(t)^\top\lambda-Q(t)^\top\lowermu_t(a_t)\\
  &\le
  B+P_0+\alpha_0-\eta\normone{Q(t)}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
If \(N\) and \(\gamma>0\) satisfy
\begin{equation}
  B+P_0+\alpha_0+\gamma\le\eta N,
\end{equation}
then whenever \(\normone{Q(t)}>N\), \eqref{eq:ec-drift-bound} implies
\begin{equation}
  \E[\Delta V(t)\mid\mathcal F_t]\le-\gamma.
\end{equation}
This is the finite-set drift certificate used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  To translate the certificate into positive recurrence
for a Markov chain, the paper also assumes a countable augmented state space,
a finite nonempty backlog sublevel set, and a closed communicating class or
equivalent local-return condition.  Without those recurrence-side conditions the theorem
should be read as a negative-drift certificate, not as an unconditional
positive-recurrence statement for every possible implementation.

\subsection*{EC.5. Statewise Feasible Families and Operational Boundaries}

The statewise corollary is a conditioning argument rather than a new drift
identity.  Let \(\chi(t)\) record scheduler-visible information such as alive
nodes, pinned tasks, queued jobs, and measured admission status.  If the full
family, candidate family, true and lower-service maps, feature map, penalty
bounds, oracle bounds, and moment bounds are indexed by
\(X(t)=(Q(t),\chi(t))\) and satisfy the same constants for every admitted state,
then the proof in EC.1--EC.4 holds after conditioning on \(\mathcal F_t\).
Uniform constants preserve both the drift and finite-horizon backlog bounds.
Positive recurrence additionally requires the augmented-state conditions in
Assumption~\ref{ass:foster-state}; it does not follow merely by averaging over
\(\chi(t)\).

The operational capacity statement is kept separate.  Sufficiency follows from
the positive-slack drift chain above once a stochastic model certifies the
arrival mean, service accounting, lower-service domination, moment bounds, and
local recurrence conditions.  Necessity is not obtained by defining a model to
``stabilize'' a load; it requires the model to encode the load and obey a
conservation law showing that long-run departures cannot exceed the capacity
base.  This separation is essential for the paper's claim boundary: Lean checks
the implication from certified assumptions to drift and recurrence, while the
experiments and admission checks certify when Scheduleurm rows satisfy those
assumptions.

\subsection*{EC.6. Variable-Duration Configuration Trajectories}

The slotted theorem does not silently cover migration, machine reconfiguration,
or port moves whose durations differ.  We therefore state the separate frame
result used by the trajectory interface.  Let \(n\) index decision frames, let
\(\mathcal F_n^{\mathrm F}\) be the information available at the start of frame
\(n\), and let the \(\mathcal F_n^{\mathrm F}\)-measurable duration satisfy
\begin{equation}
  0<\tau_{\min}\le \tau_n\le\tau_{\max}<\infty.
  \label{eq:frame-duration-bounds}
\end{equation}
The frame-boundary queue obeys
\begin{equation}
  Q^{\mathrm F}(n+1)
  =
  \left[Q^{\mathrm F}(n)-S^{\mathrm F}(n)\right]^+
  +A^{\mathrm F}(n),
  \label{eq:frame-queue-dynamics}
\end{equation}
where \(A^{\mathrm F}(n)\) and \(S^{\mathrm F}(n)\) are cumulative arrivals
and potential service during the frame.  Define
\begin{equation}
  \overline S^{\mathrm F}(n)
  =
  \E\!\left[S^{\mathrm F}(n)\mid\mathcal F_n^{\mathrm F}\right].
  \label{eq:frame-mean-service}
\end{equation}
Suppose the conditional arrival and second-order bounds are
\begin{align}
  \E\!\left[A_i^{\mathrm F}(n)\mid\mathcal F_n^{\mathrm F}\right]
  &\le \tau_n\lambda_i,
  \label{eq:frame-arrival-bound}\\
  \E\!\left[
    \frac12\sum_i
    \left\{(A_i^{\mathrm F}(n))^2+(S_i^{\mathrm F}(n))^2\right\}
    \middle|\mathcal F_n^{\mathrm F}
  \right]
  &\le B_{\mathrm F}.
  \label{eq:frame-second-moment}
\end{align}
Let \(\epsilon_{\mathrm F}\) combine candidate-support and conservative-service
losses in rate units.  The cumulative approximate-oracle and penalty obligations
are
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \tau_n H_{\Afull}\!\left(Q^{\mathrm F}(n)\right)
  &\le
  \left(Q^{\mathrm F}(n)\right)^{\!\top}
  \overline S^{\mathrm F}(n)\\
  &\quad+\tau_n(\epsilon_{\mathrm F}+\alpha_1)
    \normone{Q^{\mathrm F}(n)}\\
  &\quad+\alpha_0+P_n^{\mathrm F}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:frame-oracle}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  P_n^{\mathrm F}
  \le P_0+\tau_n\beta\normone{Q^{\mathrm F}(n)}.
  \label{eq:frame-penalty}
\end{equation}
All quantities in \eqref{eq:frame-oracle}--\eqref{eq:frame-penalty} are in
cumulative work units over the same frame; in particular, migration lost work
cannot be inserted as an unmatched wall-clock scalar.
For the controlled migration rows, let \(W_n^{\mathrm{rem}}>0\) be one task's
remaining work, let \(\underline\mu_n^{\mathrm{old}}\) and
\(\underline\mu_n^{\mathrm{new}}\) be its per-task lower-service rates in the
measured source and destination states, and let \(T_n^{\mathrm{mig}}\) be the
measured checkpoint, synchronization, staging, warmup, lost-work, and
time-equivalent risk overhead.  The migration trajectory is represented by
the duration-normalized lower service
\begin{equation}
  \underline\mu_n^{\mathrm{mig,eff}}
  =
  \frac{W_n^{\mathrm{rem}}}
  {T_n^{\mathrm{mig}}+W_n^{\mathrm{rem}}/
    \underline\mu_n^{\mathrm{new}}}.
  \label{eq:migration-effective-lower-service}
\end{equation}
It enters the candidate family only when
\begin{equation}
  \frac{W_n^{\mathrm{rem}}}{\underline\mu_n^{\mathrm{old}}}
  -
  \frac{W_n^{\mathrm{rem}}}{\underline\mu_n^{\mathrm{new}}}
  > T_n^{\mathrm{mig}},
  \label{eq:migration-dimensional-admission}
\end{equation}
equivalently
\(\underline\mu_n^{\mathrm{mig,eff}}>
\underline\mu_n^{\mathrm{old}}\).  Thus wall-clock overhead first changes the
trajectory duration; it is not subtracted directly from a service-rate score.

\begin{proposition}[Variable-duration robust frame drift]
\label{prop:frame-drift}
Suppose \eqref{eq:frame-duration-bounds}--\eqref{eq:frame-penalty} hold and the
full family has support slack \(\delta\).  Define
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{\mathrm F}
  =
  \delta-\epsilon_{\mathrm F}-\alpha_1-\beta.
  \label{eq:frame-margin}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  &\E\!\left[
    V\!\left(Q^{\mathrm F}(n+1)\right)
    -V\!\left(Q^{\mathrm F}(n)\right)
    \middle|\mathcal F_n^{\mathrm F}
  \right]\\
  &\qquad\le
  B_{\mathrm F}+\alpha_0+P_0
  -\tau_n\eta_{\mathrm F}\normone{Q^{\mathrm F}(n)}\\
  &\qquad\le
  B_{\mathrm F}+\alpha_0+P_0
  -\tau_{\min}\eta_{\mathrm F}\normone{Q^{\mathrm F}(n)},
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:frame-drift}
\end{equation}
provided \(\eta_{\mathrm F}>0\).  If the frame-boundary augmented state is
countable, its backlog sublevel sets are finite and nonempty, and the usual
local-return condition holds, then any \(N\in\mathbb N\) and \(\gamma>0\)
satisfying
\begin{equation}
  B_{\mathrm F}+\alpha_0+P_0+\gamma
  \le \tau_{\min}\eta_{\mathrm F}N
  \label{eq:frame-foster-threshold}
\end{equation}
give a finite-set Foster recurrence certificate for the embedded
frame-boundary chain.  Moreover, elapsed physical time through frame \(n\) is
at most \(n\tau_{\max}\).
\end{proposition}

\proof{Proof.}
The quadratic queue inequality applied to
\eqref{eq:frame-queue-dynamics}, followed by conditional expectation, gives
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \E[\Delta_{\mathrm F}V(n)\mid\mathcal F_n^{\mathrm F}]
  \le{}& B_{\mathrm F}
  +\tau_n\left(Q^{\mathrm F}(n)\right)^{\!\top}\lambda\\
  &-\left(Q^{\mathrm F}(n)\right)^{\!\top}
    \overline S^{\mathrm F}(n),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:frame-proof-base}
\end{equation}
where \(\Delta_{\mathrm F}V(n)\) denotes the frame-to-frame Lyapunov increment.
Full-action support slack implies
\begin{equation}
  \left(Q^{\mathrm F}(n)\right)^{\!\top}\lambda
  +\delta\normone{Q^{\mathrm F}(n)}
  \le H_{\Afull}\!\left(Q^{\mathrm F}(n)\right).
  \label{eq:frame-support-slack}
\end{equation}
Multiplying \eqref{eq:frame-support-slack} by \(\tau_n\), substituting
\eqref{eq:frame-oracle} and \eqref{eq:frame-penalty} into
\eqref{eq:frame-proof-base}, and collecting coefficients yields the first
inequality in \eqref{eq:frame-drift}.  Positivity of \(\eta_{\mathrm F}\) and
\(\tau_n\ge\tau_{\min}\) gives the second.  Equation
\eqref{eq:frame-foster-threshold} makes the conditional drift at most
\(-\gamma\) outside the finite sublevel set, so the same local-return argument
as in EC.4 applies to the embedded chain.  Finally,
\(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\tau_k\le n\tau_{\max}\) proves the calendar-time bound.
\endproof

If arrivals and service are rate-scaled throughout a frame and their unit-time
second-order term is at most \(B\), then the formal artifact also checks the
constructive choice \(B_{\mathrm F}\le\tau_{\max}^2B\).  Setting
\(\tau_n=\tau_{\min}=\tau_{\max}=1\),
\(B_{\mathrm F}=B\), and
\(\epsilon_{\mathrm F}=\epsilon_{\mathrm{cand}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{est}}\)
recovers the slotted drift theorem.  Thus the result strengthens rather than
relabels the migration boundary: a measured migration row must still certify
cumulative net service, duration, checkpoint/synchronization/resume loss, and
risk before it can enter a stochastic frame model.
